# Learning display design

This is a passive glance display. It does not claim to measure recall or replace
active study. The screen applies findings that fit incidental language exposure:

- **Adjacent glosses:** each short English gloss is directly below its Japanese
  word. This avoids a separate lookup and keeps form and meaning spatially
  associated. The natural translation is separate so omitted Japanese grammar
  remains visible. See Yanagisawa, Webb, and Uchihara (2020), *How Do Different
  Forms of Glossing Contribute to L2 Vocabulary Learning from Reading?*,
  DOI: [10.1017/S0272263119000688](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263119000688).
- **Context plus repetition:** every target appears in a short sentence, and the
  rotating display supplies repeated encounters. Spacing is more reliable than
  one massed exposure. See Cepeda et al. (2006), *Distributed Practice in Verbal
  Recall Tasks*, DOI:
  [10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354),
  and Nakata (2015), *Effects of Expanding and Equal Spacing on Second Language
  Vocabulary Learning*, DOI:
  [10.1017/S0272263114000825](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263114000825).
- **Low extraneous load:** readings, Japanese forms, and direct glosses share one
  aligned column. Only the separate natural sentence and short kanji cues are
  repeated elsewhere on the screen.

Retrieval practice has stronger evidence for durable learning than passive
rereading, including Roediger and Karpicke (2006), DOI:
[10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x),
and Karpicke and Roediger (2008), DOI:
[10.1126/science.1152408](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1152408). This device
has no learner input, so the interface does not pretend that passive exposure is
a retrieval test.

## Linguistic rules

- Gloss every lexical word, particle, and useful inflected form.
- Prefer a short grammatical gloss (`topic`, `object`, `question`) over invented
  natural English.
- Keep the fluent sentence translation on a separate line.
- Treat individual kanji meanings as memory cues, not as a compositional
  translation of the word. For example, the characters in `大丈夫` do not
  independently yield the word meaning `okay`.
- Curate context-sensitive particles and ambiguous words manually.
